 Introduction:  
Food price stability is a critical component of food security and economic well-being, particularly in developing countries such as Sri Lanka. Over recent years, Sri Lanka has experienced significant fluctuations in food prices due to economic shocks, policy changes, supply chain disruptions, climate variability, and external factors. Monitoring and analyzing food price movements is therefore essential for evidence-based decision-making, policy formulation, and early warning systems. The World Food Program (WFP) maintains a comprehensive food price database that captures market-level prices of key food commodities across Sri Lanka over time. This dataset provides a valuable opportunity to study temporal and spatial variations in food prices and to identify trends, volatility patterns, and regional disparities.
This group project aims to apply statistical and time series techniques to analyze food price behavior in Sri Lanka using WFP food price data. The study will focus on understanding price trends, seasonal patterns, variability across markets and commodities, and the potential implications for food security monitoring.
Objectives: 
Characterize the temporal behavior of food prices for selected key commodities in Sri Lanka by identifying long-term trends and seasonal patterns.
Identify spatial variations in food prices across districts and markets, highlighting regional disparities.
Examine price variability and volatility across commodities and locations to detect periods of abnormal price fluctuations.
Compare price movements across different commodity groups to understand differential market behavior.
Provide statistically driven insights that can support food security monitoring and evidence-based policy discussions.
 
About Data:  
The study will use secondary data obtained from the World Food Program (WFP) – Food Prices for Sri Lanka dataset, available through the Humanitarian Data Exchange (HDX) platform.
Key characteristics of the dataset include:
Monthly market-level prices of major food commodities
Coverage across multiple districts and markets in Sri Lanka
Variables such as commodity name, category, market location, price type, currency, unit of measurement, and time period
Data spanning several years, enabling longitudinal and time series analysis 
Research Gap
Although the WFP food price dataset has been widely used for routine monitoring, dashboards, and descriptive reporting, there is a relative lack of academically oriented statistical studies that systematically examine market-level price variability, regional heterogeneity, and volatility patterns within Sri Lanka. Most existing uses focus on national-level trends or simple visual summaries, with limited emphasis on comparative statistical analysis across districts and commodities. Furthermore, the application of structured time series decomposition and variability analysis to understand abnormal price movements in the Sri Lankan context remains underexplored. This study seeks to address this gap by applying rigorous exploratory and time series statistical techniques to generate deeper insights into spatial and temporal food price dynamics.
Analysis and Methodology:  
Data Processing and Cleaning
Identification and treatment of missing values and inconsistent records
Verification of units, currencies, and price types
Filtering and selection of key commodities and representative markets
Exploratory and Descriptive Analysis
Computation of descriptive statistics 
District-wise and commodity-wise comparisons using tables and graphical summaries
Time Series Exploration
Visualization of monthly price movements
Seasonal decomposition to identify trend, seasonal, and irregular components
Examination of price volatility over time
Comparative Statistical Analysis
Analysis of variance and non-parametric comparisons to assess differences across regions and commodities
Correlation analysis to examine co-movement of prices between selected commodities
Research Gap Identification and Interpretation
Review of existing descriptive uses of WFP food price data
Emphasis on market-level heterogeneity and volatility patterns that are often underexplored
Interpretation of findings in the context of Sri Lanka's economic and food security conditions
